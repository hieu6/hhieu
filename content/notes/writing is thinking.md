## 1. Fleeting notes

- 'Capture' thoughts, especially persistent ones to get them out of your head
- Do not need fully thought out and examined concept
- Reminders for passing thoughts in mind

## 2. Literature notes

- Main points during reading that you don't want to forget
- Be extremely selective in what you decide to keep
- Keep the overall note as short as possible
- Use your own words instead of copying quotes verbatim
- Write down the bibliographic details on the source

## 3. Permanent notes

- Review the day's fleeting and literature notes. Ideally once a day so you don't forget the context
- Consider how these notes relate to your current thinking or interests
- Don't collect ideas; develop arguments and discussions over time
- Benefit: you can look at your slipbox and see only topics that interest you
- Write as if you were writing for someone else. Full sentences, sources, references, precise and brief

### Considerations for permanent notes

- How does the new information contradict, correct, support, or add to what I already know?
- How can I combine ideas to generate something new?
- What questions are triggered by these new ideas?

## 4. Add your new permanent notes to the slip-box

- Link to and from related notes
- Add to index - table of contents and entry point for each important topic, including a sorted collection of links on the topic

## 5. Develop your topics, questions and research projects bottom up from within the slip-box

- See what is there, what is missing, questions that come up
- Look for gaps that can be filled with further reading
